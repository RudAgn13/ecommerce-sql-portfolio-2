1.1 · AOV BY CUSTOMER TIER
Average Order Value & Purchase Frequency by Tier
Bronze tier posts the highest AOV (₹39,974) — counterintuitively higher than Platinum (₹29,585). This is a sample-size effect: Bronze has only 10 customers, several of whom bought high-value electronics. In a larger dataset, Platinum should lead. Platinum does lead on avg orders per customer (1.67×), confirming the loyalty programme drives repeat purchases even if AOV is compressed by category mix. Grouped Bar · comparing two independent metrics across 4 tiers

-- AOV by customer tier
SELECT
  c.tier,
  COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id)        customers_in_tier,
  COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id)          total_delivered_orders,
  SUM(o.net_amount)                   total_revenue,
  ROUND(SUM(o.net_amount)/NULLIF(COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id),0),2) AOV,
  ROUND(1.0*COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id)/NULLIF(COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id),0),2) avg_orders_per_customer
FROM customers c
JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
WHERE o.order_status = 'delivered'
GROUP BY c.tier
ORDER BY aov DESC;

-- RFM segmentation using NTILE(5) scoring
WITH RFM AS (
  SELECT o.customer_id,
    (SELECT MAX(order_date)::date FROM orders) - MAX(o.order_date)::date recency,
    COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id) frequency,
    SUM(o.net_amount) monetary
  FROM orders o WHERE o.order_status = 'delivered'
  GROUP BY o.customer_id
), RFM2 AS (
  SELECT customer_id,
    NTILE(5) OVER (ORDER BY recency DESC) r,
    NTILE(5) OVER (ORDER BY frequency ASC)  f,
    NTILE(5) OVER (ORDER BY monetary ASC)   m
  FROM RFM
)
-- segment logic, then GROUP BY segment

1.5 · LTV BY ACQUISITION CHANNEL
Computed LTV vs Average Spend Per Customer
Paid search generates the highest computed LTV (₹10,929) driven by the longest customer lifespan (0.15 yrs avg). Referral and social have very low LTV because lifespan is near zero — most are single-order customers. Known limitation: single-order customers have lifespan = 0, zeroing out computed_ltv. A 12-month box LTV would be more robust for these segments. Bar · single continuous metric across categorical channels
View SQL▼ 
-- LTV = AOV × avg_frequency × avg_lifespan
WITH customer_summary AS (
  SELECT c.customer_id, c.acquisition_channel,
    SUM(o.net_amount) customer_revenue,
    COUNT(DISTINCT o.order_id) customer_order_volume,
    ROUND(1.0*(MAX(o.order_date)::date-MIN(o.order_date)::date)/365,2) customer_lifespan
  FROM customers c JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id=o.customer_id
  WHERE o.order_status='delivered' GROUP BY c.customer_id, c.acquisition_channel
)
SELECT acquisition_channel,
  ROUND((1.0*SUM(customer_revenue)/SUM(customer_order_volume))*AVG(customer_order_volume)*AVG(customer_lifespan),2) computed_ltv
FROM customer_summary GROUP BY acquisition_channel ORDER BY computed_ltv DESC;

SELECT
  DATE_TRUNC('month', o.order_date) order_month,
  SUM(o.gross_amount) gross_merchandise_value,
  SUM(o.net_amount) net_revenue,
  SUM(o.net_amount) - SUM(CASE WHEN o.order_status IN ('cancelled','returned') THEN o.net_amount ELSE 0 END) realized_revenue,

SELECT
  ca.parent_category, ca.category_name,
  SUM(oi.line_total) - SUM(oi.quantity*p.cost_price)
    - SUM(CASE WHEN oi.is_returned=true THEN oi.line_total ELSE 0 END) gross_profit,

WITH bucketing AS (
  SELECT
    CASE
      WHEN oi.discount_pct = 0          THEN 'No Discount'

2.5 · REVENUE CONCENTRATION — PARETO CURVE
Cumulative Revenue % vs Customer % (Top Customers)
Top 20% of customers (13 customers) account for ~75% of revenue — slightly below the classic 80/20 rule but consistent with a young customer base. The crossover where cumulative revenue exceeds 80% occurs around customer_pct 31.82% (Customer 21), meaning roughly the top 32% drive 80%+ of revenue. High concentration = high churn sensitivity among top customers. Line · Pareto/Lorenz curves require a continuous cumulative axis
View SQL▼ 
WITH customer_wise_revenue AS (
  SELECT customer_id, SUM(net_amount) revenue
  FROM orders WHERE order_status = 'delivered'
  GROUP BY customer_id
)
SELECT customer_id,
  ROUND(100.0*SUM(revenue) OVER (ORDER BY revenue DESC, customer_id ASC)/SUM(revenue) OVER (), 2) cumulative_revenue_pct,
  ROUND(100.0*ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY revenue DESC)/NULLIF(COUNT(*) OVER (),0), 2) customer_pct
-- ROW_NUMBER ensures smooth curve even on tied revenue values

SELECT
  o.shipping_state,
  COUNT(o.order_id) total_orders,
  ROUND(100.0*COUNT(CASE WHEN o.actual_delivery <= o.expected_delivery THEN o.order_id END)/COUNT(o.order_id),2) otdr_pct,

SELECT

SELECT

  COUNT(p.payment_id) total_payment_attempts,

WITH channel_expense AS (
  SELECT
    (CASE
      WHEN me.channel IN ('google')              THEN 'paid_search'
      WHEN me.channel IN ('meta','instagram')    THEN 'social'
      WHEN me.channel IN ('email')              THEN 'email'
      WHEN me.channel IN ('push','sms')         THEN 'push_sms'
    END) channel_bucket,
    SUM(me.cost_per_event) total_spend
  FROM marketing_events me GROUP BY channel_bucket
)
-- CASE WHEN mapping bridges marketing_events channel labels to customers acquisition_channel
SELECT ce.channel_bucket, ROUND(ce.total_spend/NULLIF(a.customers_acquired,0),2) cac
FROM channel_expense ce LEFT JOIN acquired a ON ce.channel_bucket=a.channel_bucket;
4.2 · EMAIL CAMPAIGN FUNNEL
Open Rate, CTR & Purchase Conversion by Campaign
Win-Back Campaign Feb leads on open rate (80%). Diwali Sale 2024 leads on CTR (83.33% of openers clicked). All three campaigns show 0% purchase conversion — this is because Diwali Sale events are in October 2024 but orders in the seed data only go to July 2024, so no orders fall within the 3-day attribution window. This is a data date-range mismatch, not a query bug — the attribution logic is correct. Grouped Bar · comparing three funnel stages across campaigns shows where each breaks down

SELECT me.campaign_name,
  COUNT(CASE WHEN me.event_type='email_sent'    THEN 1 END) emails_sent,
  COUNT(CASE WHEN me.event_type='email_opened'  THEN 1 END) emails_opened,
  COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN me.event_type='email_clicked' THEN me.customer_id END) unique_clickers,
  COUNT(o.order_id) order_within_3_days,
  ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(CASE WHEN me.event_type='email_opened' THEN 1 END)
    / NULLIF(COUNT(CASE WHEN me.event_type='email_sent' THEN 1 END),0),2) open_rate_pct
FROM marketing_events me
LEFT JOIN orders o
  ON me.customer_id=o.customer_id AND me.event_type='email_clicked'
  AND o.order_date::date-me.event_timestamp::date BETWEEN 0 AND 3
WHERE me.channel='email' GROUP BY me.campaign_name;
-- JOIN condition handles attribution; WHERE keeps all funnel rows

4.4 · CAMPAIGN ROI
Revenue Attributed & ROI % by Campaign
Google Remarketing Q1 shows 1,705,429% ROI and Meta Fashion 477,808% ROI — these are technically correct but reflect the extremely low total seed spend (₹21 and ₹9 respectively) creating a near-zero denominator. In production with realistic ad spend, ROI would be in the 200–400% range. View SQL▼ 
WITH c1 AS (
  SELECT me.campaign_name, SUM(me.cost_per_event) total_campaign_spend,
    COUNT(DISTINCT me.customer_id) customers_touched
  FROM marketing_events me GROUP BY me.campaign_name
), c2 AS (
  SELECT me.campaign_name, SUM(o.net_amount) revenue_attributed,
    COUNT(DISTINCT o.customer_id) number_of_conversions
  FROM orders o
  RIGHT JOIN marketing_events me
    ON o.customer_id=me.customer_id
    AND o.order_date::date-me.event_timestamp::date BETWEEN 0 AND 7
    AND me.event_type IN ('email_clicked','ad_clicked','push_clicked','sms_clicked')
  GROUP BY me.campaign_name
)
SELECT c1.campaign_name,
  ROUND(100.0*(COALESCE(c2.revenue_attributed,0)-c1.total_campaign_spend)/NULLIF(c1.total_campaign_spend,0),2) roi
FROM c1 LEFT JOIN c2 ON c1.campaign_name=c2.campaign_name;

WITH by_customer AS (

5.4 · NEW VS ESTABLISHED SELLER GMV

WITH seller_onboarding AS (

    EXTRACT(year FROM (SELECT MAX(o.order_date) FROM orders o)) * 12
    - EXTRACT(year FROM s.onboarded_at) * 12
    + EXTRACT(month FROM (SELECT MAX(o.order_date) FROM orders o))
    - EXTRACT(month FROM s.onboarded_at) gap
